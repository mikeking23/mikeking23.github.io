Research

I study how open-source AI can make workplace training accessible to adults with dyslexia, without requiring them to disclose it.

Much corporate training is dense, text-heavy material. For the estimated 5–17% of adults with dyslexia, that format adds cognitive load that gets in the way of learning. My work combines multimedia learning theory, cognitive load theory, and Universal Design for Learning with open-source language and video models. The goal is to turn standard training into clear, audiovisual modules that help every learner.

Poster · 2026 Asian AHRD Conference, Tokyo

Accessible Training for the Dyslexic Workforce: An Open-Source AI Blueprint

Michael King & Kevin Rose · Purdue University

This proposed study tests an HRD workflow that takes existing PowerPoint training, uses an open-source language model (Gemma) to rewrite it in plain language, and generates short training scenes with an open-source video model (LTX-2). Experts review every module before learners see it.

Research questions

  • Do dyslexic adults understand AI-generated audiovisual training better than traditional text-based training?
  • Does their confidence in understanding differ between the two formats?
  • How accurately does the pipeline reproduce the source content (errors, omissions, hallucinations)?
  • How does production effort compare with a conventional training workflow?
